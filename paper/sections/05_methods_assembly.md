## Assembly of chromosome-scale founders

Local panel selection supplies, for every marker block $b$, a panel of $R_b$ local founder rows whose alleles $y_{b\ell j}$ are either called ($0$ or $1$) or explicitly unknown. A chromosome-scale founder $k$ is a path $\pi_k=(\pi_{k1},\dots,\pi_{kB})$ that copies local row $\pi_{kb}$ in block $b$, so its allele at a site $j$ of block $b$ is $h_{kj}=y_{b\pi_{kb}j}$. A panel $\mathcal P=\{\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K\}$ of paths spans a *component* of consecutive blocks. No sample label or metadata enters assembly.

### Completion of unknown founder alleles

Before the hierarchy, a leave-bin-out ("cavity") posterior proposes fills for each block's unknown alleles. Sample diplotype weights are estimated from sites outside a 50-SNP focal bin at which every founder is called. At each focal site, the block's unknown alleles are enumerated jointly under Bernoulli(1/2) priors, exactly up to ten unknown founders and approximately above that. A fill becomes a candidate when its posterior reaches 0.99, at least two carriers support it, and its founder is distinguishable from every other founder outside the bin. An exchangeable founder therefore cannot acquire a fill from the bin alone.

An occupancy gate releases candidates only for founder rows that are demonstrably carried, judged from held-out carrier profiles of a two-fold cross-fitted variational EM over diplotypes and missing alleles [@dempster1977; @jordan1999]. A row qualifies through many confident carriers in its block, or through at least two samples that carry both the row and a uniquely matched row of an adjacent block. Folds are fixed by a deterministic hash of each sample's observation pattern, and each fold is profiled by a model fitted to the other, so no carrier profile uses the sample's own evidence. Released fills are published alleles only: every likelihood of assembly, refinement and painting, and every row distance of the count rule, reads the pre-fill calls, so a fill never feeds back into its own evidence.

### Hierarchical joining

Assembly starts with one component per block and proceeds by levels. At each level, adjacent components are grouped into batches of at most ten, and each batch with two or more components is joined into one. A boundary is made a phase break, which is never joined, when fewer than four samples are informative on both sides. A sample is informative for a component if its genotype likelihoods are non-uniform at one of the component's linking sites. Batch sizes within a break-free segment form a palindrome, so the partition is orientation-symmetric. A batch is scored only on samples informative in all its components; elsewhere a sample's state would be arbitrary.

Levels stop when one component remains, when a level after the first fails to reduce the number of components, or when only phase breaks separate components after at least four levels. After every level, each component is refined (below). Founder additions in refinement are enabled only at the terminal level, so an earlier level's founder count is the working set of the next join.

### Join objective

For sample $i$, site $j$ and an unordered pair $\{k,l\}$ of panel paths, the emission is the robust, centred log predictive likelihood

$$\epsilon_{ij}(k,l)=\max\Big\{\log\Big(0.99\sum_{g}\bar p_{ij}(g)\Pr(g\mid h_{kj},h_{lj})+\frac{0.01}{3}\Big),\,-2\Big\}-\log\frac13,$$

where $\bar p_{ij}(g)=P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g)/\sum_{g'}P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g')$. $\Pr(g\mid h_{kj},h_{lj})$ is a point mass at $h_{kj}+h_{lj}$ when both alleles are called. An unknown allele is treated as Bernoulli(1/2), and two copies of one unknown local row share one latent allele. Contamination and floor bound any single genotype's influence. Emissions are summed over the usable sites (kept sites with a called founder) of bins $t=1,\dots,T$ that never cross a block, giving $E_{it}(k,l)$. Each input component gets about 20 bins of 10–100 sites, with at most 2,000 bins per batch. The per-sample score is a Viterbi maximum over sequences of unordered pairs [@rabiner1989],

$$V_i(\mathcal P)=\max_{z_1,\dots,z_T}\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{T}E_{it}(z_t)-\lambda\,\#\{t>1:z_t\neq z_{t-1}\}\Big],$$

with a free start and a uniform penalty $\lambda$ per state change. $\lambda$ is 20 nats up to 5,000 spanned sites and $20\sqrt{L_{\mathrm{span}}/2000}$ beyond that, where $L_{\mathrm{span}}$ is the number of sites the batch spans. The join log likelihood $\mathrm{LL}(\mathcal P)=\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}V_i(\mathcal P)$ sums over the $\tilde N$ informative samples $\mathcal I$ of the batch. LL compares equal-size panels; the count rule compares sizes.

### Candidate paths and panel search

Candidate paths come from a linker. For every gap $\delta$ up to a horizon $G$, it fits haploid founder-transition matrices between components $b$ and $b+\delta$ by EM [@baum1970; @dempster1977]. Homologues move independently between components; within a component, at most one homologue switches between adjacent sites, and a two-state error-tract process absorbs short discordant runs. $G$ covers every gap at level one. At later levels, $G=\max\{1,1+\lfloor 0.5/(3\bar D)\rfloor\}$, where $\bar D$ is the mean component span in Morgans. An endpoint beam scores a path by summing, over components, the mean fitted log transition into the component from earlier components within the horizon. It keeps 16 paths per endpoint founder. The linker and beam only propose; the horizon never censors a term of LL.

The panel search starts from the best "anchor" panel, formed for one block from the first beam path through each of that block's rows. It alternates same-count moves with count steps. Tier 1 ranks bounded families of edits by fixed-painting gains: replacement by a pool path, local row changes, one-sided copies, suffix splices and cyclic exchanges. It scores the leading edits of each kind exactly and accepts the best improvement. At a tier-1 stall the count step runs; once it is quiescent, tier 2 bounds every move of an explicit neighbourhood and scores the survivors exactly, best bound first.

A same-count move must leave every changed path with a private cell (a block row no other path uses) and more than 1% mismatch to every other path. Otherwise the move would create a founder that the count rule deletes without having decided it. Finally, the beam is rerun with doubled endpoint quotas, up to 256 paths, or 1,024 if a move was accepted at 256. A panel is certified when no quota yields an improving move or an accepted birth. Certification is local to this neighbourhood and pool, not global.

### Founder-count criterion

One rule decides every founder count in joins and refinement. Let $\mathcal P^{+}$ and $\mathcal P^{-}$ differ by one founder $f$, and let $d_i$ be the per-sample gain of $\mathcal P^{+}$ under $V_i$ (joins) or $F_i$ (refinement, below). Each founder costs $c=\gamma\tilde N L_{\mathrm{span}}/200$, with $\gamma=0.5$ and $\tilde N$, $L_{\mathrm{span}}$ taken from the batch or component decided. A BIC-style rule [@schwarz1978] would charge this against every sample, but a founder's evidence accrues only in the samples that carry it. The rule therefore charges $c/(2\tilde N)$ per effective carrier, measured by the participation ratio of the positive gains [@kish1965]:

$$n_{\mathrm{eff}}(d)=\frac{\big(\sum_i d_i^{+}\big)^2}{\sum_i (d_i^{+})^2},\qquad m(d)=\frac{\sum_i d_i}{c}-\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}(d)}{2\tilde N},$$

where $d_i^{+}=\max(d_i,0)$. A gain vector passes when $m(d)>0$ and $m'(d)>0$, where $m'$ omits the sample of largest gain ($\tilde N$ unchanged), so one large carrier cannot replace independent support. With $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tilde N$, the margin is the BIC threshold $\sum_i d_i/c-\tfrac12$.

The total gain alone does not suffice, because a recombinant of existing founders gains likelihood by saving switches without contributing new sequence. The rule therefore also tests the sequence part $q_i$: the score of $\mathcal P^{+}$ minus that of its shadow $\mathcal P^{+\sigma}$. The shadow replaces each block row of $f$ that a reference panel lacks by the reference's nearest row there, using Hamming distance on co-called pre-fill sites. $\mathcal P^{+}$ is kept, or accepted, only if both $d$ and $q$ pass. A founder without a private cell is a pure mosaic: it has $q\equiv0$ and is never kept. Equally near rows define a Cartesian family of shadows, evaluated completely up to 2,048 members. If the family's verdicts are opposed, the smaller count is chosen and the call is flagged ambiguous. A larger family is evaluated through its first member only, and the unchanged decision is flagged count-unverified. Each decision carries a bootstrap band: individuals are resampled 2,000 times with a deterministic, content-keyed stream, and the 95% percentile interval of $\min\{m(d),m(q)\}$ is recorded [@efron1993]. A band containing zero in its interior marks the call as ambiguous but never reverses it.

In a join, deaths precede births and the shadow reference is the smaller panel. Paths without novel sequence (no private cell, or within 1% mismatch of another path) are deleted unconditionally. Otherwise the death candidate with the largest $\mathrm{LL}(\mathcal P\setminus f)$ is tested. If that founder is kept, the novel pool path with the largest accepted gain is added; per-sample upper bounds first screen out paths that cannot pass. Pure-mosaic pool paths that would pass on total gain suggest that one panel path covers two founders' sequence; that path is copied, both copies are refitted, and the result is accepted if it survives its own death test and raises LL. Deleting a path born in the same search, or returning to an earlier count state, closes a count cycle: the deletion stands and births stop.

### Founder refinement

Refinement re-optimises each component at fixed $K$, then revisits the count. Each homologue of sample $i$ follows its own founder chain between consecutive informative sites, with transition $(1-\rho_{ij})\mathbf I+\rho_{ij}\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\top}/K$. The reset probability is $\rho_{ij}=1-\exp(-\Delta_{ij})$, where $\Delta_{ij}$ is the genetic distance in Morgans since the sample's previous informative site. Resets are thus Poisson along the map without interference, as in @haldane1919, and a reset changes founder with probability $\rho_{ij}(1-1/K)$. Relative to staying, a change costs $\kappa_{ij}=\log\{1-\rho_{ij}(1-1/K)\}-\log(\rho_{ij}/K)$, and

$$F_i(\mathcal P)=\nu_i(K)+\max_{(k_j,l_j)_j}\sum_{j}\Big[\epsilon_{ij}(k_j,l_j)-\kappa_{ij}\big(\mathbb 1[k_j\neq k_{j-1}]+\mathbb 1[l_j\neq l_{j-1}]\big)\Big]$$

is the maximum-path value of this copying model [@li2003], taken over every informative site without binning. The normaliser $\nu_i(K)=-2\log K+\sum_j 2\log\{1-\rho_{ij}(1-1/K)\}$ sums over the sample's informative sites after the first. It is panel-independent but depends on $K$, so it enters every comparison across sizes. Because both homologues share $\rho_{ij}$, the recursion reduces exactly to the $K(K+1)/2$ unordered pairs. $F=\sum_iF_i$ is a robust score, not a posterior probability.

At fixed $K$, each sweep has three steps:

1. Compute the panel's exact forward and backward messages.
2. Propose, for every founder and every window of eight blocks, the exact maximiser of a binned surrogate of $F$ with the other founders fixed. From level two, windows of four first-level spans also propose whole first-level paths.
3. Walk the block junctions, scoring exactly every founder-pair suffix exchange and every proposed move, and accept those whose gain exceeds $2^{-9}$ nats.

Sweeps alternate direction until one accepts nothing, a local optimum of this move set; each fit runs from both directions and keeps the higher $F$. Moves spanning first-level spans are guarded. If such a move worsens the emission fit and raises $F$ only through saved switches, it must still clear the threshold once the savings at crossovers shared between samples are removed. Descendants of one meiosis share its breakpoint, and without the guard a founder could absorb that crossover and become an artificial recombinant.

The count pass refits the incoming panel, then runs deletion rounds while $K>2$. Each round refits the panel without each candidate founder (only those lacking a private cell, if any) and decides the best refit; the first rejection ends deletions. Addition rounds follow only at the terminal level. Each candidate is the panel plus a copy of one founder, refitted at $K+1$, and additions continue until one is rejected or $K$ doubles. Refinement uses a symmetric null: the shadow reference is the larger panel minus each member of its own best unrefitted death class. The same two panels thus get the same decision whichever was the incumbent; disagreement among members selects the smaller $K$ with ambiguity. Joins treat paths within 1% mismatch as one sequence, whereas refinement, whose terminal decisions are final, scores the sequence part exactly. The decisions choose $K$, and within it the best converged fit of the pass is kept. The call is flagged ambiguous if that fit fails its own deletion test. A final polish (beam escapes, long repairs, exchange bridges) can raise $F$ without changing $K$ or revisiting the count.

### Ties, alternatives, release and approximations

Continuous comparisons use tie classes whose width is a power-of-two quantum scaled to the score. Inside a class, joins consult linker evidence and then content order. A class that the data cannot separate is represented by the smaller founder count and then the fewest exact switches. Its other members are retained as families of alternatives. A later join evaluates every joint choice of its inputs' families, provided there are at most 16, on a common cohort and site set, and compares outcomes by LL within each founder count. At export, founder rows are cut wherever a retained family's panels disagree on continuity. Alleles are withheld at blocks where those panels disagree on content, and wherever no painted carrier has observed evidence. Count-unverified decisions and unperformed searches are recorded as metadata, never as biological alternatives.

With more than nine count candidates, only the nine best-ranked are refitted, so a successful candidate can be missed at large $K$. Shadow families and branches have explicit budgets, and a window search that exhausts its work account returns the component unchanged, recording the unperformed search. Unknown founder alleles enter every likelihood through a predictive approximation for each observation, not through joint integration across samples.
