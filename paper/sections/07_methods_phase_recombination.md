## Family phase, recombination maps and released outputs

### Family phase conditional on the inferred pedigree

Family phase re-estimates every sample's two founder-label paths jointly with its released relatives, and releases genotypes, haplotype phase, phase sets and one transmission track per parent-to-child edge (a *meiosis chain*). It conditions on frozen upstream products: the painting's bins, states, estimated label-switch probabilities and tempered emissions; the founder allele refit; the released (Tier B) parent edges; and the genetic map. It refits none of them, and neither family phase nor the recombination maps feed back into any earlier stage. Each assembled component is solved separately, so no phase is carried across a component boundary.

**State space.** Let $x_{i\sigma b}\in\{1,\dots,S\}$ be the painting state ("label") of homologue $\sigma\in\{0,1\}$ of sample $i$ in painting bin $b=1,\dots,B$. The states are the painting's classes of founder rows that are identical over its selected sites, plus a BACKGROUND state ($S=K+1$ when every row forms its own class). When the pedigree releases a parent $p$ for slot $\sigma$ of sample $i$, that homologue also carries a source $v_{i\sigma b}\in\{0,1,\bot\}$, an inheritance indicator as in multipoint linkage analysis [@lander1987]. For $v\in\{0,1\}$ it copies parental homologue $v$, so $x_{i\sigma b}=x_{pvb}$; for $v=\bot$ ("not a copy") its label is free, absorbing painting or panel errors, conversion, mutation or a false edge. A homologue without a released parent is a free label path. Slots are conventions: slot 0 and slot 1 carry no sex or parental-role meaning, and no sample metadata enters the model.

**Transitions.** Between bins $b-1$ and $b$, whose centres (the mean positions of their sites) lie $\Delta_b$ bp and $d_b$ Morgans apart (supplied map, or a fallback uniform rate), the model uses a label-switch probability $\theta^{\ell}_b$, taken from the painting's estimated copying process; a meiosis-switch probability given by the Haldane map function [@haldane1919],
$$\theta^{m}_b=r_H(d_b),\qquad r_H(d)=\tfrac12\left(1-e^{-2d}\right);$$
and not-a-copy entry and exit probabilities $\alpha_b=1-\exp(-\rho_\bot\Delta_b)$ and $\beta_b=1-\exp(-\Delta_b/\mu_\bot)$, where $\rho_\bot$ is an entry rate per bp and $\mu_\bot$ a mean run length in bp. A free label keeps its label with probability $1-\theta^{\ell}_b$ and moves to each other label with probability $\theta^{\ell}_b/(S-1)$; we write this law $T^{\ell}_b(x\to x')$. A linked homologue moves on the augmented state $z\in\{0,1\}\cup(\{\bot\}\times\{1,\dots,S\})$ with
$$
T^{\mathrm{link}}_b(z\to z')=
\begin{cases}
(1-\alpha_b)\,\big[(1-\theta^{m}_b)\,\mathbf{1}(z'=z)+\theta^{m}_b\,\mathbf{1}(z'\neq z)\big], & z,z'\in\{0,1\},\\
\alpha_b/S, & z\in\{0,1\},\ z'=(\bot,x'),\\
(1-\beta_b)\,T^{\ell}_b(x\to x'), & z=(\bot,x),\ z'=(\bot,x'),\\
\beta_b/2, & z=(\bot,x),\ z'\in\{0,1\},
\end{cases}
$$
and starts in either copy state with probability $(1-\omega)/2$, or in $(\bot,x)$ with probability $\omega/S$, where $\omega=\rho_\bot\mu_\bot/(1+\rho_\bot\mu_\bot)$ is the stationary not-a-copy occupancy. A homologue entering $\bot$ draws its label uniformly rather than from the copied label, so that changing a parent's labels never alters a descendant's transition terms; this keeps block updates exact. Across user-supplied protected intervals (reference-order breakpoints, and both ends of reversed segments) $\theta^{m}_b$ is set to $1/2$, so transmission is unlinked there.

**Objective.** With $e_{ib}(x,y)$ the painting's binned log emission of the unordered label pair, the stage maximises
$$
J=\sum_{i}\sum_{b}e_{ib}(x_{i0b},x_{i1b})
+\sum_{(i,\sigma)\ \mathrm{free}}\ \sum_{b\ge2}\log T^{\ell}_b(x_{i\sigma,b-1}\to x_{i\sigma b})
+\sum_{(i,\sigma)\ \mathrm{linked}}\Big[\log\nu_0(z_{i\sigma1})+\sum_{b\ge2}\log T^{\mathrm{link}}_b(z_{i\sigma,b-1}\to z_{i\sigma b})\Big],
$$
where $\nu_0$ is the initial law above. The emission is the painting's robust log-likelihood of the bin's reads, $P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g)$, under the genotype distribution implied by the two labels, divided by the painting's design-effect factor for correlated sites within a bin. $J$ is therefore a tempered log joint posterior of all labels and sources, up to a constant, not an exact likelihood. The emissions use the painting's pre-refit allele model, so the allele refit does not change this ancestry objective.

**Optimisation.** $J$ is maximised by exact block coordinate ascent, one block per sample. Sample $i$'s block holds both slot paths and, when $i$ has children, a binary gauge path $\zeta_{ib}$; $\zeta_{ib}=1$ reads every child's source for $i$ flipped at bin $b$. One block thus contains every move that exchanges the names of $i$'s homologues from some bin onwards without breaking its children's transmissions. With all other samples fixed, the block is a hidden Markov model with $n_0n_1G$ states ($n_\sigma=S$ for a free slot, $S+2$ for a linked slot; $G=2$ with children, otherwise 1). Its emissions add the emissions of every descendant whose labels copy $i$'s homologues; its transitions add its children's chain transitions. The Viterbi recursion [@rabiner1989] solves the block exactly.

The ascent starts from each sample's free-label Viterbi path on its own emissions; samples with a released parent are then re-solved ancestors first, so that parental origin orients them. Sweeps visit samples ancestors first and keep a block's new path only when it raises $J$ by more than a small gain quantum, computed exactly from the terms of the samples it changes. The ascent is monotone and stops when a sweep changes nothing, or at a sweep cap, which is recorded as unsettled. A converged ascent need not reach the global optimum.

Block moves alone cannot fix one configuration. Where both homologues of a sample without a released parent change label at the same bin edge, its own unordered-pair emissions cannot tell which old label continues into which new one; only its children can. A child copying the arbitrary pairing absorbs it by switching its other homologue, which no single block can undo. A joint move therefore exchanges the parent's homologues from that edge onwards, propagates the copied labels and re-solves each child's block. It is kept only when $J$ rises by more than $\log[\tau_s/(1-\tau_s)]$, the odds of the set-continuation threshold $\tau_s$ defined below; a pairing whose alternative lies within that margin is recorded as ambiguous and breaks phase sets. The margin is a working value against one optimised alternative, not a calibrated pairing posterior.

Arbitrary conventions (visit order within a pedigree depth, tie-scan direction, descendant order) can decide ties, so the optimisation is run twice, independently, under opposite conventions. For fixed upstream painting, founder-refit and pedigree inputs, release requires the two solves to agree on a call or phase join. Reversing conventions elsewhere in the pipeline can change those upstream inputs, so this within-stage agreement does not guarantee convention-invariant whole-run output.

**Release of genotypes and phase.** For each sample and solve, with all other samples fixed at that solve's optimum, the exact forward–backward recursion [@baum1970; @rabiner1989] over the same block gives posterior probabilities $\pi_{ib}(x,y)$ of the ordered label pair. These posteriors are conditional on the rest of the pedigree's optimal configuration; they are not joint marginals. At a released site $j$ in bin $b$ (a refit panel site inside a painting bin), a named state's allele $\hat h_{xj}$ is known only when all rows of its class carry the same called refit allele; BACKGROUND has no allele. Posteriors of the ordered alleles $(u_0,u_1)$ carried by homologues 0 and 1 are
$$
P_{ij}(u_0,u_1)=\sum_{x,y:\ \hat h_{xj},\hat h_{yj}\ \mathrm{known}}\pi_{ib}(x,y)\,
\frac{w_{xyj}(u_0,u_1)\,P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g=u_0+u_1)}{\sum_{u_0',u_1'}w_{xyj}(u_0',u_1')\,P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g=u_0'+u_1')},
\qquad
w_{xyj}(u_0,u_1)=(1-\delta)f_{xj}(u_0)f_{yj}(u_1)+\delta\,U(u_0,u_1).
$$
Here $f_{xj}$ gives allele $\hat h_{xj}$ probability $1-d_{xj}$ and the other allele $d_{xj}$, where $d_{xj}$ is an empirical-Bayes estimate of the panel allele's uncertainty from the other carriers' reads and a chromosome-wide prior, with the sample's own direct term removed. The mixture mass $\delta$ redraws the ordered pair from a uniform genotype ($U=1/3,1/6,1/6,1/3$ for $00,01,10,11$), representing a label error shorter than a bin, conversion or mutation. Posterior mass on pairs with an unknown allele is dropped, not renormalised, so such pairs withhold calls. A genotype is called from the unanchored posterior when its probability reaches $\tau_g$ (default 0.9). A called heterozygote is phased when $\max(P_{ij}(0,1),P_{ij}(1,0))\ge\tau_p$ (default 0.99), and is otherwise released unphased.

A sample with a released parent is oriented by parental origin: homologue $\sigma$ is the one transmitted from its slot-$\sigma$ parent. A sample without released parents but with children is oriented through its children's chains (the gauge). A sample with neither has no anchor beyond convention, so each partition (opened by a forced break or by the end of the previous set) is anchored at its first bin whose label pair is heterozygous with probability at least $\tau_p$, by requiring homologue 0 to carry the smaller label index there. The posterior is recomputed under that constraint, and the set ends at the first later heterozygous bin whose orientation certainty falls below $\tau_s$ (default 0.999). Orientation certainty is the median, over the bin's sites called heterozygous with probability at least $\tau_g$, of $\max(P_{01},P_{10})/(P_{01}+P_{10})$, with $P_{u_0u_1}=P_{ij}(u_0,u_1)$. Uncertainty about which labels a sample carries does not end a set where the candidate labels carry the same alleles. The stricter $\tau_s>\tau_p$ reflects that a wrong continuation misphases the rest of a set, whereas a wrong site call misphases one site.

Phase sets also break where the data cannot fix relative phase. The first case is painting tie boundaries in samples without a released parent, where the painting's track order was a convention; a sample with children bridges such a break when its children-anchored orientation certainty reaches $\tau_s$ on both sides. The second is identity junctions. A founder row is a trajectory through assembled blocks, not a certified biological homologue, so two rows can exchange identity at a block junction. Carriers of one row then switch labels there, whereas carriers of both keep continuous labels whose content changes homologue, which their own emissions cannot reveal. Where the free or not-a-copy tracks of at least two samples switch between the same two labels, a sample carrying both labels on both sides has its phase join withheld, unless a linked homologue copies one parental homologue across the edge and the parent's own reads pin that homologue's content on both flanks. The third is ambiguous pairings, which break the parent's and its children's sets. Breaks from both solves are combined. A genotype is released where both solves call the same genotype, and a heterozygote is phased only where both phase it; a new set starts wherever the solves' relative orientation changes, and the default solve's allele order is released. A phase set asserts relative phase within itself only; the orientation of separate sets is arbitrary.

**Meiosis chains.** For each edge $e$ from parent $p$ to slot $\sigma$ of child $i$, the block posterior gives, per bin, the probability $\kappa_{eb}(v)$ that the child's homologue copies parental homologue $v$. Chains reset at the component start, across protected intervals and at either individual's phase-set starts. Parental homologue order is slot order for a parent with a released parent, and otherwise each solve's gauge, aligned between solves by the parent's labels within each reset segment. A source is released where both aligned solves give $\kappa_{eb}(v)\ge\tau_p$ for the same $v$, with support $\bar\kappa_{eb}$, the smaller of the two solves' maxima.

### Conditional recombination maps

Recombination maps are estimated from the released meiosis chains, conditional on the fixed inferred pedigree, the final phase and the supplied genetic map. An optional table of reference-order breakpoints removes the sites of every painting bin overlapping a breakpoint interval from the map axis and splits the component there, because painting and phase are rigid within a bin; this prevents misassembly or inversions from appearing as crossover pile-ups.

For each edge, released chain bins are markers at bin centres $c_m$ with sources $s_m$. Consecutive markers within the same reset-free stretch define an interval $(c_{m-1},c_m]$ with parity $o_m=\mathbf{1}(s_m\ne s_{m-1})$. An odd interval is a called crossover, reported with the outer bounds of its two bins and a joint endpoint support $\max(0,\bar\kappa_{m-1}+\bar\kappa_m-1)$. Unreleased bins between markers are bridged but unresolved, so an even number of crossovers between markers cannot be seen; a lone marker contributes nothing, and no interval crosses a reset. Under Haldane's no-interference model, the number $\xi$ of crossovers in an interval of genetic length $\lambda$ is Poisson with mean $\lambda$, and odd parity has probability $r_H(\lambda)$. Each interval therefore contributes the conditional expectation
$$
\mathbb{E}[\xi\mid o=0]=\lambda\tanh\lambda,\qquad \mathbb{E}[\xi\mid o=1]=\lambda\coth\lambda\ \ (\to1\ \text{as}\ \lambda\to0),
$$
with $\lambda$ taken from the supplied map at the bin centres, or from a fallback uniform rate without a map. It is spread over the interval by genetic length (uniformly in bp without a map or genetic length), and the interval adds one meiosis of exposure per bp. Both expectations tend to $\lambda$ as $\lambda$ grows, and to the called parity as $\lambda\to0$. The estimate thus follows the called transmissions where markers are dense and reverts to the supplied map across long uninformative intervals. Called parity enters as data; the estimate is not a posterior computed from the chain model.

Expected crossovers $X_t$ and exposure $W_t$ (meiosis-bp) are summed over edges in fixed-width map bins $t$, split at component and breakpoint boundaries. The rate is $10^8X_t/W_t$ cM/Mb and is missing where $W_t=0$: uninformative sequence is not evidence of absent recombination. Observed genetic length accumulates rate times covered bp over the union of informative intervals only, so it omits gaps. Called crossovers per unit of callable exposure are reported alongside. A report-only detector groups overlapping calls into loci and flags patterns of transmission change that ordinary recombination does not explain; it never masks or refits the map.

### Released outputs

For each contig, export writes a founder VCF and a phased-sample VCF [@danecek2011] on the same rows: by default every input biallelic SNP that reached the site filter, removed sites listing their removing rules in FILTER. The founder file has one haploid column per founder row of each released assembly segment. Labels are local to a segment, so no column asserts identity across segments. Alleles that are unknown, ambiguous or without read support from a painted carrier, and positions outside the segment's span, are `.`; at sites removed only as uninformative, the strict read-count majority allele may fill unblocked rows. The sample file holds the family-phase calls as GT:PS in input sample order, without imputation. PS is the position of the phase set's first site; an unphased heterozygote is written `a/b` with missing PS without ending its set, and filtered sites are `./.`. Recombination tables report per-meiosis calls, exposure and map bins in the units above.


Each table family is written once per run as CSV with snake_case columns; chromosome-resolved
rows have a leading `contig` column and follow configured contig order. Per-chromosome files
are limited to the VCFs and optional `export/<contig>.tracks.csv.gz`; recombination produces one
multi-page `recombination/maps.pdf` and one `recombination/coverage.csv` of spans between
consecutive released markers of a followed stretch, shared by map aggregation, junctions and
evaluation.
