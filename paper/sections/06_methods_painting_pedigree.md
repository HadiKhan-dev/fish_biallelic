## Painting, pedigree inference and founder refit

### Painting samples as diploid founder mosaics

Each assembled component, a span of blocks sharing one founder panel, is painted for every sample as an independent hidden Markov model (HMM), so local ancestry restarts at every assembly break.

**States and emissions.** Painting uses the component's selected sites, those where at least one founder row is called. Rows whose calls (unknown calls included) agree at every selected site are indistinguishable by any sample and are pooled into one trajectory class. With $K' \le K$ classes, each homologue ("track") is in one class or in a BACKGROUND state that stands for a haplotype absent from the panel, giving $S = K'+1 \le K+1$ states. A class is anchored if it is called at some selected site. The ALT probability $q_{uj}$ of state $u$ at site $j$ is the called allele $h_{kj}$, or, for an uncalled class and for BACKGROUND, $f_j=(1+n^{\mathrm{alt}}_j)/(2+n^{\mathrm{call}}_j)$, the Beta(1,1) posterior mean of the class calls at the site. Selected sites are split into $B=\max(1,\lfloor L_s/100\rfloor)$ near-equal consecutive bins. With normalised genotype likelihoods $\tilde\ell_{ij}(g)=P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g)/\sum_{g'}P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g')$, the tempered bin emission of sample $i$ in state pair $(u,v)$ is

$$
e_{ib}(u,v)=\frac{1}{D_{ib}}\sum_{j\in b,\ \text{observed}}\left[\max\left\{\log\Big((1-\epsilon)\sum_{g}\tilde\ell_{ij}(g)\,P_j(g\mid u,v)+\tfrac{\epsilon}{3}\Big),\ \ell_{\min}\right\}-\log\tfrac13\right],\qquad D_{ib}=1+(n_{ib}-1)_+\,r .
$$

Here $P_j(g\mid u,v)$ is the genotype law of two independent alleles with ALT probabilities $q_{uj}$ and $q_{vj}$, except that two tracks of one named class share a latent allele and so cannot be heterozygous. The genotype-error mixture is $\epsilon=0.01$, the floor is $\ell_{\min}=-50$ nats, and $n_{ib}$ counts the sample's informative sites in the bin (observed sites with non-flat likelihoods). The divisor $D_{ib}$ is a design effect [@kish1965]. A product over sites treats them as independent given the copying pair, which overstates a bin's evidence, because panel errors run along rows and read errors are correlated between nearby sites. The correlation $r=0.1$ is a constant tuned on simulations and sparse-read controls, not an estimate.

**Copying chain.** In the manner of the copying model of @li2003, each track's label jumps at rate $\rho$ per Morgan, uniformly to one of the other $S-1$ labels. Between bin centres $d_b$ Morgans apart, a track therefore changes label with probability

$$
\theta_b=\frac{S-1}{S}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{\rho\,d_b\,S}{S-1}\right)\right],
$$

floored at $10^{-15}$, and the two tracks change independently. Each anchored class also has a panel-error state, which keeps the founder label but emits as BACKGROUND, representing a locally wrong row or a haplotype locally missing from the panel. While a label persists, its fidelity follows a two-state chain that enters error with probability $\alpha$ and leaves it with probability $\beta$. After a label change, fidelity is drawn from the chain's stationary law. The error state lets a track keep its founder label through a locally wrong row, paying an entry and an exit cost rather than two ancestry changes; painted switches count label changes only. For each component, $(\rho,\alpha,\beta)$ are estimated from all samples with an informative site by eight expectation–maximisation updates [@baum1970; @dempster1977] from fixed starting values $(2,10^{-3},0.25)$, each an exact scaled forward–backward pass [@rabiner1989] followed by bounded maximisation. This is a deterministic, truncated estimator, not a test of convergence.

**Decoding and release.** Each sample is decoded by exact Viterbi recursions under two traceback conventions (the chain and its time reversal), which maximise the same score but break exact ties from opposite ends. Exchanging the two tracks is a gauge choice, not a switch. Where classes $a$ and $b$ have identical genotype laws at every site a sample observes over a run of bins, the data cannot place a switch from $a$ to $b$ within the run, so its bins carry the candidate set $\{a,b\}$ (a switch interval). Release is gated by two posterior masses with both tracks copying faithfully: $q^{F}_{ib}$, of the Viterbi unordered class pair, and $q^{AF}_{ib}$, of the corresponding pair of local allele-equivalence classes (classes with identical calls in the bin). Each mass is the smaller of its values for the two conventions' Viterbi pairs. With $\tau=0.9$, a bin with $q^{F}\ge\tau$ releases both tracks as their classes. Otherwise, a bin qualifies when $q^{AF}\ge\tau$ and every track whose local class pools several classes is directly supported by an informative site where its class is called. Such tracks are then released as their local class, and the other track as its class. Otherwise both tracks abstain.

### Label continuity

Where rows are locally interchangeable, assembly can let two founder rows exchange identity at a link point between blocks while the allele set at every site stays correct. Painting, however, reads label continuity as haplotype continuity. Every carrier of either haplotype then either switches at that point or exchanges its tracks, which flips a parent's phase frame and puts a false crossover into each child. Before the final decode, each component is therefore offered suffix transpositions, which exchange the labels of two singleton anchored classes from a link point onwards and so change emissions only in the bin straddling the link. Max-product messages of the label-level chain (without error states) give each sample's exact best-path gain for every transposition. The gains are summed over samples in units of one track change, and candidates gaining at least two units are ranked. The screen only ranks: each candidate is decided by re-decoding every sample under the full model. It is accepted when the total painted switch count falls by at least two and the samples whose count rises number at most 0.1 times those whose count falls. Under a genuine label exchange, the only losers are carriers with a real haplotype change inside the link interval. Under a real crossover inherited by one lineage, every other carrier loses a switch, so the ratio rule keeps such crossovers in the rows, even when the recombinant lineage is the majority. The search repeats until no candidate is acceptable, and the relabelled panel is kept only if repainting it strictly lowers the switch total. No pedigree, label or truth enters.

### Pedigree inference

**Candidates.** Every sequenced sample is a child target, and every other sample is a candidate parent. Sample names are identifiers; no generation, cohort, sex or breeding metadata enters. A child's configuration is M0 (no observed parent), M1 (one candidate $p$) or M2 (an unordered pair $\{p,p'\}$); parent slots carry no sex. A slot without an observed parent follows a null source representing an unsequenced or unidentified parent, not a named individual.

**Transmission model.** A sample's source chain is an HMM over its ordered diplotypes, $S^2$ states, with painting's tempered emissions and label-level transitions. Its exact posterior marginals are symmetrised over homologue order. A parent transmits one homologue per bin, through a selector that switches between bin centres with the Haldane recombination fraction $r_b=\frac12(1-e^{-2d_b})$ [@haldane1919], as in the inheritance vectors of @lander1987. Because the exact transmitted-state law is an $S^4$ object for two parents, we project it onto a first-order chain that keeps the exact marginal $\pi_{pb}(s)$ of the founder state transmitted by candidate $p$ in bin $b$. Between bins, the chain uses a maximum-entropy transition matrix matching the exact adjacent marginals and the exact probability that the transmitted state persists [@csiszar1975]. Next to a parent bin with no informative markers, where the painted path is only interpolated, each bridge $T$ is replaced by $(1-r_b)T+r_bU$, with $U$ uniform. Named classes transmit their panel allele (or $f_j$ where uncalled); only BACKGROUND is conditioned on the candidate's reads, because the panel leaves alleles uncalled where data are noisy and conditioning on equally noisy reads would penalise true parents. The null source of child $c$ starts from, and redraws from, the frequency of transmitted founder states among the other available samples, with the child left out:

$$
\phi_{cb}(s)=\frac{\sum_{i\ne c}A_i\,\pi_{ib}(s)+w_0/S}{\sum_{i\ne c}A_i+w_0},
$$

where $A_i=1$ if sample $i$ has evidence in the component and $w_0=1$. The null slot keeps its state across an edge with weight $\kappa_b=(1-r_b)\big(1-\tfrac{S}{S-1}\theta_b\big)$ and otherwise redraws from $\phi_{c,b+1}$.

**Composite score.** The child's state in a bin is the ordered pair of founder states transmitted into its two slots, each following a parent's projected chain or, when unobserved, the null source. Up to ten evenly spaced markers per painting bin are scored. With transmitted ALT probabilities $x$ and $y$, a marker contributes

$$
P_{cj}(x,y)=3\sum_g\tilde\ell_{cj}(g)\,P\big(g\mid x',y'\big),\qquad x'=\varepsilon_{\mathrm m}+(1-2\varepsilon_{\mathrm m})x,
$$

with $y'$ defined likewise and mismatch probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm m}=0.01$. Each configuration $\mathsf a$ is scored by two forward passes [@rabiner1989]. One uses full-strength emissions. The other is tempered, raising each bin's emission to $\eta_{cb}=\min(3,n)/n$, where $n$ counts the child's markers with non-uniform likelihoods in the bin's information group (consecutive bins holding at least 100 markers). The chromosome score is the composite

$$
\ell_{cz}(\mathsf a)=\tfrac12\left[\log L^{(\eta)}_{cz}(\mathsf a)+\log L^{(1)}_{cz}(\mathsf a)\right],
$$

summed over components. It pools two views of correlated evidence and is not an independent-marker likelihood. Certified upper bounds decide where M1 is evaluated exactly. Candidates far below the child's best genome-wide M1 are not retained and share a floor profile. A pair of retained candidates enters the score-defined M2 panel when a valid upper bound on its M2 comes within 150 nats of the child's best known score, in the full fit or in any leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) fit. Pairs outside the panel contribute nothing, so M2 evidence is a lower bound.

**Genome-wide decision.** For chromosome multiplicities $w_z$, the genome evidence of a non-M0 configuration is mixed once with M0:

$$
\mathcal L_c(\mathsf a)=\log\left[(1-\gamma)\,e^{\lambda\sum_z w_z\ell_{cz}(\mathsf a)}+\gamma\,e^{\lambda\sum_z w_z\ell_{cz}(\mathrm{M0})}\right],\qquad\gamma=0.02,
$$

so a configuration that fails on part of the genome cannot score more than $\log(1/\gamma)$ below M0. The scale $\lambda\le1$ maximises the predictive probability of held-out chromosome folds and is applied only when an interior value below one gives positive gain; it is an evidence weight, not posterior calibration. A configuration is testable only when its edges (and an M2 pair jointly) are exposed, in posterior expectation, in at least one bin, in 10% of painted bins, and on chromosomes holding three map units (Morgans of measured map, at least one per chromosome). Otherwise it contributes M0 likelihood to its state and cannot be named.

Direction is inferred from the data: a parent's painted junctions are inherited along the homologue it transmits, so per chromosome we compute the probability that the parent's inherited, detected junctions do not exceed the child's count. The two directions' log ratio is mixed with a chromosome-level painting-failure probability of 0.05 and summed over chromosomes. Three composite family terms are then added to every configuration: a direction term, bounded by a pair-level failure probability of 0.02; a reciprocal factor that excludes $p$ being $c$'s parent while $c$ is $p$'s, solved by converged cavity messages (loopy belief propagation [@murphy1999]); and a factor against reverse two-edge ancestry paths, computed over each sample's 16 best configurations.

State evidence is the log mean likelihood over a state's configurations, using the full number of eligible configurations (1, $N-1$, $(N-1)(N-2)/2$) plus a uniform state prior. The parent-count state is chosen first, then the identity within it. A winner within a numerical tie tolerance is unresolved. Local choices are made acyclic by a bounded greedy repair that only moves a child to an equal or lower parent count, so the graph never creates parent evidence. Support comes from refitting everything downstream of the chromosome scores ($\lambda$ fixed) on 1,000 chromosome bootstrap resamples [@efron1979] and on $C$ LOCO fits.

**Release.** Tier B, the primary release, releases a parent count when its bootstrap support reaches 0.7 and its LOCO support 0.8, when the child's informative chromosomes (those on which its own evidence has a unique winner) hold three map units, and when an M0 call persists under alternative state priors $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$ and $(0.2,0.3,0.5)$. For M1 and M2, some local parent must also be stable. This withholds counts that persist while the winning candidate changes, as with relatives of an unsequenced parent. An exact configuration further requires the local and graph configurations to agree, configuration and parent support, and direction log odds of at least $\log 49$ on every edge. Tier A uses 0.95 throughout. Edges that are stable and directed on their own can be released as marginal edges. An unresolved identity is reported as a candidate set of the fewest configurations covering 95% of the within-state probability.

**Sibling-as-parent guard and count release.** Both steps use a full-marker Mendelian exclusion e-value [@shafer2021; @vovk2021], computed from genotype likelihoods at jointly observed sites and never tempered. A dyad is excluded when $\log E\ge\log(2N/\alpha)$, with $\alpha=0.01$. A parent of $c$ is a parent of every full sib of $c$, and so is Mendelian-compatible with each of them. A relative wrongly released as $c$'s parent is expected to be excluded from most of $c$'s other sibs. For each released Tier-B edge $p\to c$, the guard takes $c$'s full-sib family from the relatedness product, without $p$, without members joined to the family by released edges, and, as a half-sib heuristic, without members with a different released parent. It withholds the edge when at least two members remain and a strict majority of them are excluded from $p$. Withdrawals iterate to a fixed point, are never reversed, and return the child to unresolved. An unsequenced shared co-parent can veto a true edge, so an absent edge is not evidence that the parent is absent. For children whose Tier-B count remains unresolved, a count of zero or one observed parent is released when, after removing candidates whose reverse direction is supported, at most one unexcluded candidate remains and it agrees with the supported and acyclic-view parents.

Each child's release is thus an exact configuration with zero, one or two observed parents; a stable count with a candidate set; marginal edges with count bounds; edges withheld for unresolved direction or by the guard; or an unresolved state. A complete acyclic best-guess view is reported but is not a release.

### Founder allele refit

Refit reconsiders individual founder alleles with the released painting labels held fixed. At each site, samples whose two tracks are released as singleton rows $\{a,b\}$, and that are observed in the founder-construction view, are pooled into a table of untempered robust log-likelihoods, $\mathcal T_j(a,b,g)=\sum_i\log\big((1-\epsilon)\tilde\ell_{ij}(g)+\epsilon/3\big)$. The table gives exact gains, other rows held fixed, for three moves: a single-row flip; a swap of the different alleles of two co-painted rows, which corrects an allele carried by the wrong row; and a joint flip of their equal alleles. Each site runs three sweeps of coordinate ascent. A move is accepted only when it gains 5 nats for each allele it changes, a hysteresis that gives the assembled allele prior odds of about 150:1. Within a round, qualifying moves are judged against the round's starting state and applied in order of gain, provided they do not interact. Two interacting moves of one tie class, where applying one would stop the other from qualifying, are alternatives the data cannot separate. Their rows' alleles are released as unknown rather than decided by row order. After the sweeps, alleles the panel left unknown are filled when one allele gains 5 nats and at least two carriers' own reads favour it. Pedigree inference never reads the refit panel.
