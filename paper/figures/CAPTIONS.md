# Draft captions and figure placement for P2

The manuscript now targets release 4.3. Figures retain the versions actually measured (4.1 and 4.2); the planned 39-case 4.3 matrix is not represented as completed validation here.

Figures 1 (pipeline) and 2 (kernel ladders) are unchanged. Figures 3–12 are generated by `tools/make_results_figures.py`, exclusively from `data/`. Every CSV has portable source references; `data/provenance.json` defines extraction scope. Suggested main figures: 3, 6, 7, 9 and 12, with 11 alongside the performance results. Figures 4, 5, 8 and 10 can be supplementary. Dense figures 3, 5–7, 9 and 10 should use full page/two-column width; do not shrink the sample identifiers into one journal column. The smaller panels of 4, 8, 11 and 12 can be rearranged by the journal without changing their data.

## Figure 3 — Accuracy across simulated designs

**Draft caption.** Accuracy of release 4.2 in all 25 scored simulation cases, grouped into 15 designs. Each row is one completed matrix run; variants of the same design reanalyse the same simulated data and are not independent biological or simulation replicates. Stars identify K = 30; diamonds identify the compound stress design. (a) One-to-one founder called-allele errors per million called founder alleles, summed over final `founder_refit` components/chromosomes before division. (b) Genotype errors per million called diploid genotypes. (c) Phase orientation switches per million correctly called true heterozygotes released phased. Switches are counted between consecutive scored sites within each released phase set. (d) Phased true heterozygotes divided by correctly called true heterozygotes. (e) Called sample alleles divided by total sample alleles. (f) Correct, missed and extra inferred pedigree edges against simulation truth, as counts. Correct plus missed equals the number of truth edges among observed samples; extra edges are additional calls. All extra-edge counts here are zero. Designs with no observed truth edges are labelled; they do not demonstrate successful parent recovery. Panels a and c use a symmetric-log scale with a linear interval from zero to one; all axes include zero. The stress n80 simulation has 80 generated and 64 observed samples. The stress design changes observation fraction, read error, depth dispersion, allelic bias, dropout and backcrossing together, so it does not isolate depth or missingness effects.

**Data:** `data/simulation_accuracy.csv` (4.2 rows); design and variant definitions in `data/cases.csv`. Extracted from each simulation's `evaluation/summary.json`, `evaluation/chromosomes.csv` (the chromosome records are also in summary.json), and `evaluation/founders.csv`; design settings from `final/configs/<case>.toml`. Ratios are ratios of summed counts, not means of chromosome rates. Phase errors exclude genotype errors. See Figure 12 for the 4.1 comparison.

## Figure 4 — Final founder component recovery (stage coverage limitation)

**Draft caption.** Founder accuracy and count recovery at the evaluated endpoint. (a) Each point is a final `founder_refit` component from one reference run per simulation design; small vertical offsets separate points and carry no quantitative meaning. The horizontal axis is the component's one-to-one called-allele errors per million called founder alleles (symmetric-log scale, linear from zero to one). (b) Reconstructed founder rows against the number of truth founder haplotypes, with the equality line. Coincident points overlap. K = 30 and stress are marked as in Figure 3. Correct founder counts do not establish correct founder sequence recovery.

**Data:** `data/founder_components.csv` (4.2, reference variants), extracted from `evaluation/founder_components.csv`.

**Important limitation:** The matrix evaluations contain **only `founder_refit`** in `founders.csv`, `founder_components.csv` and `founder_matches.csv`. There are no truth-scored discovery, selection, assembly or pre-refit panels in this evidence. This is an endpoint/component figure, not a stage-by-stage improvement curve. Stage timing or refit change counts cannot substitute for missing stage accuracy scores. `data/provenance.json` lists the available stages for every evaluated case and release.

## Figure 5 — Perturbation agreement, with the convention exception exposed

**Draft caption.** Direct within-release agreement of perturbation runs with their own design's unperturbed reference, separately for releases 4.1 and 4.2. Rows cover sample-order permutation, two iid numerical perturbation magnitudes (one retained seed each), validation subsampling, VCF reordering, and tie-convention reversal for TroMau, actm3, standard n80 and stress n80. Grey dashes mark variants not present in the final matrix. Blue equals signs denote identical compared products; orange unequal signs denote at least one differing file in that output family. This is agreement of released labelled outputs, not an accuracy score. A difference may include founder/phase relabelling under the alternate convention; it must not be interpreted automatically as a biological error. In particular, TroMau chr3's released segment structure is convention-sensitive in 4.2 (Figure 8c), and the orange reversal row must remain visible.

**Data:** `data/robustness.csv`; exact patterns and retained columns in `data/provenance.json`. Comparisons are variant versus reference **within each release**, computed from run outputs, not inferred from the cross-release scores. Founder count comparisons retain component, physical bounds, founder rows and count ambiguity, excluding search/count diagnostics. Paintings compare every field in `painting/*/sample_segments.tsv`; pedigree compares all `tier_*relationships.csv` including partial calls; relatedness compares all published CSVs; phase summary compares the released sample/site/component, called-allele, phased/unphased-heterozygote and phase-set counts in `family_phase/summary.csv`; recombination compares every published CSV. Founder and sample VCF agreement compares the column header and all records after removing `##` metadata, including founder alleles and missingness, and sample GT, phase separators and PS. Sample rows and VCF columns are aligned by identifier. Relatedness families are labelled by their member sets, unordered parent slots and undirected pair endpoints are canonicalized, and whole painting-track exchanges within a sample/component are allowed. Tables use exact string/discrete values and relative tolerance 10⁻⁹ and absolute tolerance 10⁻⁸ for numeric cells, with paired NaNs equal; `elapsed_seconds` is omitted. The recombination `parent_slot` display index is omitted, while parent and child identifiers are retained. Founder identities are not optimally rematched under reversal, and the sample VCF comparison retains the released within-phase-set orientation; those two columns are convention-sensitive representation checks rather than gauge-free biological equivalence tests. `differing_files` identifies every file failing that stated comparison. The release-segment column additionally compares manifest intervals, founder-row counts and withheld-allele counts, excluding search diagnostics. These selected product families do not include every diagnostic, export manifest annotation or identity-support table. `data/release_comparisons.csv` separately records the existing 4.2-versus-4.1 score inventories, including diagnostic changes, and is not used as a substitute for within-release comparisons.

## Figure 6 — TroMau chromosome 1 founder mosaics

**Draft caption.** Released founder paintings on TroMau chromosome 1 for all 116 samples. Two adjacent thin tracks per sample show the two painted homologues; track order is an arbitrary painting frame and is not a maternal/paternal assignment. Each colour identifies a named founder row H0–H7 in this chromosome's painting component. Grey hatching pools local allele equivalence, posterior abstention, switch intervals and background; these regions do not assert one named founder. Segments are drawn at their reported half-open physical intervals `[Start, End)`, without smoothing or interpolation. Samples are ordered by decreasing inferred relatedness-family size, then family membership identifiers; adjacent rows are not asserted known relatives. Sample names are identifiers only, and neither cohort nor sex labels enter the ordering. Founder colours identify component-local reconstructed haplotypes, not known sequenced ancestors and not identities that can be carried across chromosomes.

**Data:** `data/tromau_mosaic.csv` from the reference run's `painting/chr1/sample_segments.tsv`, retaining every segment, status and candidate-row set. `data/tromau_samples.csv` supplies identifiers and the deterministic inferred-family ordering from `relatedness/families.csv`. This shows painting-stage segments, not a re-derivation of the final family-phase VCF. No real-data truth or accuracy is claimed.

## Figure 7 — TroMau recombination landscape and crossover burden

**Draft caption.** Recombination output across all 22 analysed TroMau chromosomes. Each upper panel shows the released `rate_cM_per_Mb` over the physical intervals of the map table, with a common vertical scale including zero; rates are not smoothed or rebinned. Hatched grey marks unavailable-rate bins and does not mean zero recombination. (b) Called crossovers per reported parent–child meiosis, summed across the 22 chromosome tables; one histogram entry per parent–child/slot combination, including entries with zero called crossovers. (c) Total called crossovers on each chromosome divided by the number of reported meiosis rows on that chromosome. These estimates are conditional on the followed/released chains and their observable exposure, which varies among meioses; the histogram is not a direct count of all biological crossovers in the cross. Rates are inferred map rates, not simply the histogram of called crossover intervals. No generating/true map is available for this real dataset.

**Data:** `data/tromau_recombination.csv` (all 859 released map bins, including one unavailable-rate bin) from `recombination/<chr>.map.csv`; `data/tromau_meioses.csv` (1,540 chromosome–meiosis rows) from `recombination/<chr>.meioses.csv`. The latter reports 70 genome-wide meioses and 874 called crossovers. These recombination chains contain one parent–child edge beyond the 69 edges in the Tier B relationship table used in Figure 9; do not silently identify the two edge universes. All computations are directly from the retained counts and exposure columns, without imputing absent meioses.

## Figure 8 — TroMau founder counts and released segment structure

**Draft caption.** Inferred founder counts and component-local release structure in TroMau. (a) One point per exported founder release segment in the 4.2 reference run, with small vertical offsets for overlapping points; the horizontal line spans the minimum and maximum count on that chromosome. Counts are not summed over segments because founder identity resets at release boundaries. (b) Physical spans of exported release segments, labelled by the number of founder rows; hatching indicates ambiguity in the corresponding assembly founder count. These spans show supported output segmentation, not proven chromosome-wide biological identity. (c) TroMau chr3 release segments in both releases, under reference and reversed conventions; parentheses give segment counts, and orange boundary marks expose the short middle segment even when its width is hard to resolve. Both conventions give three chr3 release segments in 4.1; 4.2 gives one in the reference and three under reversal. Thus one 4.2 reference component is not evidence that the convention dependence has been eliminated.

**Data:** `data/tromau_founder_counts.csv`, extracted from `export/manifest.json` (`founder_components`, positions and `source_founder_rows`) for each release/convention, joined to `painting/<chr>/founder_counts.tsv` by source component for count ambiguity. The latter table describes assembly components and alone would conceal release-segment splits. In the 4.2 reference, 24 exported segments span 22 chromosomes; 17 segments have eight founder rows, five have nine and two have seven. Those are segment counts, not numbers of independent biological founders. H0 across release segments or chromosomes is not the same inferred identity.

## Figure 9 — TroMau inferred family and pedigree structure

**Draft caption.** Family and parentage inference for the TroMau samples, without individual-level pedigree truth. (a) Distribution of inferred relatedness-family sizes, including singleton families. (b) Tier B released parent states: zero, one or two observed parents, or unresolved; zero observed parents does not mean the individual has no biological parents. (c) An adjacency display of all Tier B observed parent–child edges: rows are inferred children with at least one released observed parent, columns are the corresponding inferred parent identifiers, and a blue cell is a released edge. Rows follow the inferred-family ordering used in Figure 6, with separators at family changes. Parent slots are unordered; names do not imply cohort, sex or biological parentage. Samples with zero observed parents or unresolved state are counted in b and omitted from the edge matrix. (d) Selected graph configuration support for those child rows, showing chromosome bootstrap and leave-one-chromosome-out support on the full 0–1 scale. These support values quantify internal inference stability and are not calibrated accuracy probabilities; configuration support is not the same quantity as individual-edge support.

There are 116 samples in 72 inferred families. Tier B has 68 zero-observed-parent, 25 one-observed-parent, 22 two-observed-parent and one unresolved state; the matrix displays 69 edges for 47 inferred children and 14 distinct inferred observed parents. Family membership and all parent assignments are inference. No cohort labels or assumed G0 parentage are used as truth.

## Figure 12 — K = 30 founder recovery and downstream trade-offs

**Draft caption.** K = 30 simulation before and after the 4.2 natural-domain count-refit change, comparing the scored 4.1 and 4.2 matrix outputs. Each simulation contig has one evaluated final `founder_refit` component. (a) Component-wide nearest-row called founder allele errors, not the one-to-one metric of Figures 3–4. (b) Chimeric segment charges, defined by the evaluator as component-matched called-allele errors minus segment-wise errors; they are mismatch charges, not counts of crossover events or independent chimeras. (c) Reconstructed founder row counts with truth counts marked by horizontal black ticks. On m2, 32 reconstructed rows become the true 30, called founder errors fall from 1,431 to 603, and chimeric charges fall from 620 to 22. Other contigs remain imperfect; the release does not recover every truth founder everywhere. (d) Aggregate genotype, phase and pedigree metrics show the mixed downstream consequences, including the increase in switch errors and decrease in correct pedigree edges. The changes compare complete releases, not a new isolated causal experiment or biological replication.

**Data:** `data/founder_components.csv` and `data/simulation_accuracy.csv` (K = 30, both releases), from `evaluation/founder_components.csv` and `evaluation/summary.json`. One-to-one founder errors are also retained in the CSVs; do not substitute nearest-row errors for the one-to-one results. The measured cost of the additional refits is shown in Figure 11.
