# S5. Family phase, recombination maps and released outputs

## Family phase

Notation follows the main text. Parameter names are those of the generated parameter reference; values are 4.3.0 defaults.

**Emissions, released sites and state alleles (FP01, FP13).** The pair emissions $e_{ib}(x,y)$ are the painting's binned emissions, read from its cache or recomputed identically with its `robustness_epsilon` (0.01), `emission_log_floor` (−50 nats) and design effect $1+(n_{ib}-1)\rho_s$. Here $n_{ib}$ counts informative sites of sample $i$ in bin $b$ and $\rho_s$ is `emission_site_correlation` (0.1). Both solves share these pre-refit emissions. Released sites are refit panel positions inside a painting bin; other sites keep missing calls. A named state's allele $\hat h_{xj}$ exists only where every row of its class has a called refit allele and all agree; BACKGROUND has none. Components without painting bins are not solved. They keep their geometry (fallback bins of `EMPTY_COMPONENT_BIN_SITES` = 100 panel sites), but their calls and chain sources stay missing.

**Edge rates (FP02).** $\theta^{\ell}_b=1-\sqrt{\psi_b}$, where $\psi_b$ is the painting's pair stay probability, clipped to $[10^{-15},(S-1)/S]$. The other clips are $[10^{-12},1/2]$ for $\theta^{m}_b$, $[10^{-12},1/2]$ for $\alpha_b$ and $[10^{-6},1-10^{-6}]$ for $\beta_b$. `not_copy_rate_per_bp` $\rho_\bot=10^{-8}$ and `not_copy_mean_bp` $\mu_\bot=5\times10^{4}$ give $\omega\approx5\times10^{-4}$. One Haldane implementation (via `expm1`) serves every map distance. `[family_phase] protected_intervals` is a TSV (`chrom, start, end, reason`; 1-based inclusive). Ordinary rows protect their span; a `reversed_segment` row protects its two endpoints. An edge whose closed centre-to-centre interval overlaps a protected interval gets $\theta^{m}_b=1/2$.

**Local gains (FP03, FP25).** Because $\bot$ entry draws a uniform label, $J$ is a sum of per-sample terms, each reading only that sample's labels and sources. A move's gain is the accurately rounded sum of after-minus-before terms over exactly the samples it changed (the moved sample and its descendants); no change gives exactly zero. This local gain decides acceptance. The full $J$, recorded per sweep, is a verification quantity that may round differently near a threshold. A test-only reference path compares the two and never alters production arithmetic.

**Block recursion, ties and order (FP04, FP05).** The block transition is contracted in three stages (previous source of homologue 0, previous source of homologue 1, previous gauge). This costs $O(B(n_0^2n_1G+n_0n_1^2G+n_0n_1G^2))$, plus $O(B\,|\mathrm{desc}|\,S^2)$ for descendant emissions. Only gauge 0 is allowed at the first bin. In each stage a candidate replaces the incumbent if its score beats the retained best by more than `tie_quantum` ($10^{-9}$ nats), or lies within it and is the slot's current state (for the gauge, the unchanged gauge). The retained argument's score, not the numerical maximum, continues the recursion. At the final bin, the current sources with gauge 0 win ties. Samples are ordered by depth (longest ancestor chain), then index; the graph must be acyclic. These orders fix the Gauss–Seidel updates.

**Initialisation and sweeps (FP06, FP07).** The first pass solves every sample in index order with both slots free and no child terms, from all-zero labels. The second pass solves samples with a released parent in ancestor order, initially copying parental homologue 0. Neither pass applies a gain test. Sweeps keep a block only if its local gain exceeds `gain_quantum` ($10^{-6}$ nats), restoring it otherwise, for at most `max_sweeps` (30) sweeps. A component is settled when the final sweep of both solves accepted nothing; the recombination summary reports this as `family_phase_converged`.

**Pairing moves (FP08).** Candidates are samples with no released parent and at least one child, at every bin edge where both of their homologues change label. They are enumerated once per pass in pedigree then bin order. A move that changes nothing is skipped. A move is kept if its gain exceeds $\max(\log[\tau_s/(1-\tau_s)],\texttt{gain\_quantum})$, which is 6.907 nats at $\tau_s=0.999$. Passes with kept moves trigger more sweeps, for up to `max_sweeps` passes. At that cap, a final non-keeping pass measures ambiguity in the final state. A pairing is ambiguous when the alternative's gain exceeds $-\log[\tau_s/(1-\tau_s)]$.

**Convention solves (FP09).** The mirrored solve reverses three orders: visits within a depth, the scan direction for non-incumbent ties, and descendant order during ascent. It keeps the incumbents, gauge preferences, thresholds and first-pass order. Neither solve initialises the other. The default solve's orientation is released even when the run's reporting convention is reversed.

**Identity junctions (FP10).** A candidate is an edge $j$ and labels $\{a,b\}$ at which at least `junction_min_samples` (2) distinct samples have a free track, or a track not-a-copy at $j-1$ or $j$, switching between $a$ and $b$. A copied homologue's switch belongs to its parent or is a crossover, so it is not counted. A sample carrying $\{a,b\}$ on both sides of $j$ is pinned only under three conditions. A linked homologue must copy parental homologue $v$ at both $j-1$ and $j$. The parent must not itself carry both labels. On each flank of up to `junction_pin_bins` (10) bins, stopping where the parent's labels change,
$$\textstyle\sum_{b'}\big[e_{pb'}(x,y)-\max_{y'}e_{pb'}(z,y')\big]\ge\texttt{junction\_pin\_nats}\ (10),$$
with $x$ the label on homologue $v$ and $z$ the exchanged label. The partner $y'$ is free because two rows exchanged together can fit the same unphased reads. Children never pin: a child's copy excludes the alternative only through a meiosis switch exactly at $j$, whose odds depend on the interval and on uncertainty the children share. Withheld joins of both solves are combined and cannot be bridged.

**Painting ties and forced breaks (FP11, FP12).** At each boundary between a sample's released painting chunks, direct and swapped continuations are compared lexicographically by (label changes, allele-visible changes). The boundary is a tie unless direct continuation is strictly better. It is mapped to the next site's bin and kept only at internal bins of samples with no released parent. Forced breaks combine tie bins, withheld joins and ambiguous-pairing edges (applied to the parent and all its children) from both solves. Only tie-only cuts can be bridged.

**Panel-allele uncertainty (FP14).** The prior is the logit of the chromosome-wide share of allele 1 among called refit cells, clipped to [0.01, 0.99] (`PRIOR_BOUNDS`). Carriers of $x$ at site $j$ are samples whose optimal label pair contains $x$; samples flagged by sample quality control are excluded, though they are still phased. Each carrier adds $\ell(g_1)-\ell(g_0)$, where $\ell(g)=\log[(1-\varepsilon)P(r_{ij},a_{ij}\mid g)+\varepsilon/3]$, $\varepsilon$ is the painting's `robustness_epsilon`, and $g_u$ is the carrier's dosage if $x$ carries allele $u$ given its partner's selected allele; an $(x,x)$ carrier compares dosage 2 with 0. With $\gamma_{xj}=\mathrm{logistic}(\text{prior}+\Lambda_{xj}-\Lambda^{(i)}_{xj})$, where $\Lambda_{xj}$ sums the carrier terms, $d_{xj}$ is the disagreement of $\gamma_{xj}$ with the selected allele, capped at 1/2 (`FLIP_CEILING`) and zeroed below $10^{-15}$. The sample's own direct term $\Lambda^{(i)}_{xj}$ is removed for the labels of its optimal pair. Documented approximations remain. This is not a cross-fitted refit, and the sample can still influence carrier and panel selection. The two copies flip independently even for $(x,x)$, and correlated carrier identity errors remain a failure mode.

**Posterior, site model and calls (FP15–FP17).** Forward–backward uses the Viterbi factors, starts the gauge at 0 and enforces any anchor in both directions. States with log posterior ≤ −700 are dropped without renormalisation. Descendant sums follow a fixed (depth, index) order in both solves. Chain probabilities stay in float64 until the release test. Settings are refused unless $\tau_s<(1-5\delta/6)/(1-2\delta/3)$, the orientation certainty of a fully determined heterozygote (0.99983 at $\delta=10^{-3}$). They also require $\tau_p\le\tau_s$, all thresholds in $(1/2,1)$ and $\delta\in(0,1/2)$. `site_deviation` ($\delta$) and `set_posterior` ($\tau_s$) are working values chosen on validation simulations, not estimated error rates. Genotypes use the argmax dosage, with the lower dosage winning exact ties, at the inclusive `genotype_posterior` ($\tau_g$ = 0.9). Heterozygotes are phased at the inclusive `phase_posterior` ($\tau_p$ = 0.99). The order is (1,0) only if $P_{10}>P_{01}$, otherwise (0,1). Calls carry both alleles or neither.

**Gauge-sample sets (FP18).** These rules apply to samples with neither a released parent nor children. The anchor is the first bin of a partition whose optimal labels differ and whose label pair is heterozygous with posterior ≥ $\tau_p$. Certainty is computed over whole bins up to the next forced break. The set stops at the first later bin with differing optimal labels, label heterozygosity ≥ $\tau_g$ and certainty < $\tau_s$. Undefined certainty (no confidently heterozygous site) does not stop it. Pre-anchor bins join the anchored partition; each stop opens a new partition.

**Children-anchored gate (FP19).** For a sample without released parents but with children, a tie-only cut is bridged when the certainties at the nearest informative bins before and at-or-after the cut both reach $\tau_s$. A cut lacking such a bin on one side is kept. A break kept by either solve is kept.

**Agreement (FP20).** Breaks are combined, and a set also starts wherever the relative orientation of the two solves changes between consecutive phased heterozygotes. The first site is never a break.

**Chain release (FP21).** Resets fall at the component start, wherever $\theta^{m}_b\ge1/2-10^{-12}$, and at the bins of either individual's agreed set starts. A start inside a bin (after its first released site) blocks that bin and resets the next. $\kappa_{eb}(v)$ excludes not-a-copy mass. For a parent without a released parent, alignment uses the bins of each reset segment where the default label pair is heterozygous and the unordered pairs match. If all those bins are swapped, the mirror is flipped; if only some are, the segment is blocked. Bins whose aligned parental labels still differ are blocked. Supports are stored as float32, zero where unreleased.

**Storage and product (FP22–FP24).** Resident or windowed storage, fixed 4,096-site chunks and worker count do not change calls, phase sets or chains. Each product binds the exact parent-edge digest it was conditioned on. Painted-span coverage is diagnostic, and recombination does not read it.

**Cost in K.** A sweep costs $O(B(NS^3+S^2\sum_iA_i))$, with $A_i$ one plus $i$'s descendant count. A pairing move costs $O(n_cBS^3)$ for $n_c$ children. Release costs $O(NB(S+2)^3G)$ for posteriors, $O(LS^2)$ per sample for the site model, and $O(EB)$ for chains. Emissions take $8NS^2B$ bytes. Every term is at most cubic in $K$ ($S\le K+1$).

## Recombination maps

**Breakpoints (recombination RC01, RC02).** `[recombination] order_breakpoints` uses the protected-interval TSV format; the two settings are independent. Ordinary intervals (in file order), then reversed-segment endpoints as point intervals, are made half-open as $[\text{start},\text{end}+1)$. Each is widened cumulatively to every overlapping painting bin and removed from the map axis, with a new segment after it. Inversion interiors are not masked automatically, and calls inside reversed segments are flagged. A chain bin spanning two segments or a masked zone is invalid. Segment changes reset chains.

**Following (recombination RC03).** A marker is a released bin in a valid segment; stretches restart at any chain or segment reset. A called interval is $(c_{m-1},c_m]$, between bin centres. Its reported interval is the inclusive bp range of both bins, so a change inside either bin is not mislocated. The joint lower bound $\max(0,\bar\kappa_l+\bar\kappa_r-1)$ holds under any dependence. For each meiosis the stage counts released, followed and unresolved bins.

**Conditional map (recombination RC04, RC05).** Map bins start every `bin_bp` (1,000,000 bp) from the first site, with extra edges at the two sites flanking each segment change. $\lambda$ is the difference of cumulative Morgans interpolated at bin centres. Map knots between the end sites are retained, so hotspots between SNPs keep their mass. The fallback rate `[genetic_map] rate_cm_per_mb` (default 5 cM/Mb) applies without a map and beyond its knots. An odd interval of zero genetic length contributes one crossover: the data override the map's zero prior. Edge sums follow a fixed order. The columns are:

- `rate_c_m_per_mb` is $10^8X_t/W_t$, missing where $W_t=0$.
- `covered_bp` is the union of all meioses' informative intervals within the bin.
- `c_m_on_covered_bp` is rate × covered bp / $10^6$; the cumulative sum counts missing values as zero.
- `effective_meioses` is $W_t$ divided by the map-bin width.
- The called rate divides called crossovers (spread by genetic mass, else uniformly in bp) by callable exposure.

`rate_is_measured` does not alter the calculation.

**Junction diagnostic (recombination RC06).** Overlapping or touching reported intervals form loci, and a locus is admitted when at least `minimum_parents` (3) distinct parents have calls in it. Each edge is scored by the parity of its calls in the locus. It is decidable if it has a call there or a callable span covering the calls' extent. A parent with $\eta_1$ odd of $\eta$ decidable meioses contributes $\log[\tfrac12q^{\eta_1}(1-q)^{\eta-\eta_1}+\tfrac12q^{\eta-\eta_1}(1-q)^{\eta_1}]$. Here $q$ is the probability of a real transmission change, and the mixture is over a frame flip (prior 1/2) shared by the parent's children. Three statistics follow.

- **Mixing.** The maximum over 500 values $q\in[0.001,0.5]$, minus the maximum over $q\le q^{\max}$. The ceiling is $q^{\max}=q_{\mathrm{pt}}+r_{\mathrm{bg}}-2q_{\mathrm{pt}}r_{\mathrm{bg}}$, with `hotspot_switch_probability` $q_{\mathrm{pt}}=0.05$ and $r_{\mathrm{bg}}$ the Haldane fraction across the locus.
- **Pairing.** For each locus pair, a discordance mixture over 50 values $\phi\in[0.005,0.25]$, among parents decidable at both loci, minus the larger independent switching maximum. The best partner is kept, minus $\log(\text{locus pairs tested})$.
- **Flag.** A locus is flagged when the larger statistic reaches $\log100$ (`flag_log_likelihood_ratio`).

The documented rationale is that no locus gives real crossovers in more than 5% of meioses beyond background, with flags at 100:1. Ties take the first maximum, or the last under reversed conventions. The ratios are conditional on the calls, not calibrated odds, and never mask the map.

**Stage and cost (recombination RC07).** The stage refuses chains conditioned on another parent-edge digest. Its cost has no intrinsic $K$ dependence: $O(EB)$ following over the $B$ chain bins, $O(EB_m+\text{calls})$ aggregation over $B_m$ map bins, and a detector cost of $O(\text{loci}^2E)$ plus $O(\text{loci}^2\times\text{parents}\times\text{grid})$.

## Released outputs

**Site rows (OU01).** By default (`[export] full_length = true`) export writes every input biallelic SNP reaching the site filter, in position order. Multiallelic, non-SNP and duplicate-position records dropped by the loader are absent. Kept sites have FILTER `PASS`; removed sites list their rules in r1–r5 order; a kept-only export writes `.`.

**Founder VCF (OU02–OU04, OU06).** Column `cC_Hk` holds row $k$ of release segment $C$ as a haploid GT, and is `.` outside the segment's panel span. Upstream (OU03), a called allele is withheld unless an eligible painted carrier supplies observed read support, using refit calls. At sites removed only by the informativeness rule r5, unwithheld rows receive the strict majority of summed REF and ALT allele depths (GT only where AD is unusable). Ties stay `.`, and the fill carries no founder-specific evidence. Sites removed by r1–r4 stay `.`. Founder-identity flags mark duplicate-partner changes between blocks, and are diagnostic only.

**Sample VCF (OU05, OU09).** Columns are in input order. A set starts at a component's first kept site and at the site after each released break; its PS is that bp position, which need not be heterozygous. Inside a set, homozygous and missing cells also use `|` and PS. A frame-undetermined heterozygote is written `/` with ascending alleles (missing first) and PS `.`, without ending its set. Sites outside components are unphased, removed sites are `./.:.`, and there is no r5 fill.

**Recombination tables (OU18).** Run-level `recombination/crossovers.csv`, `meioses.csv`,
`map.csv`, `coverage.csv`, `junctions.csv` and `summary.csv` use a leading `contig` column and
snake_case fields; call intervals are $(\text{left},\text{right}]$. The single coverage table
contains spans between consecutive released markers within a followed stretch, used by map
aggregation, called exposure, junctions and evaluation. `recombination/maps.pdf` has one page per
chromosome in configured contig order, presenting the same conditional map as the tables.

**Identity and optional tracks.** `export/founder_identity.csv` is the run-level identity table,
with `contig`, `component` and `founder` identifiers. Optional per-chromosome
`export/<contig>.tracks.csv.gz` files use `contig`, `component`, `pos` and `hk` columns; shorter
segments leave unused founder columns empty, while an existing unknown allele remains `.`.
The per-chromosome VCF paths and their sample/founder column identifiers are retained.

**Cost.** The sample VCF costs $O(NL)$ and the founder VCF $O(L\times\text{columns})$ over $L$ written sites, at most quadratic in $K$.
